# Try it

## Change this title and watch the slide
### The slide on the right follows every word you type

Click into this title on the left and write something of your own. There is
nothing to drag or resize: you write the words, and the theme lays them out.

Undo always works, and nothing is saved to the file until you press Save.

> [!TAKEAWAY]
> You write the argument. The theme does the formatting.

## One file does three jobs
### The same text is your slides, your document and your source

:::: metrics
#### Slides
**Present**

What you show in the meeting.

#### Document
**Read**

What people read before or after it. Switch with Document and Slides above the preview.

#### Source
**Keep**

Plain text that you and the agent edit together, and that lives in Git.
::::

## Add a layout by picture
### Press Add at the top, then choose Compare options

:::: comparison
#### Before
A blank slide and a set of boxes to line up by hand.

#### After
Two clean columns, already on-brand, waiting for your words.
::::

> [!TAKEAWAY]
> Pick the layout by what you want to argue, not by how it looks.

## Ask the agent for the heavy lifting
### Say what you want in the chat; it edits this same file

- "Make the slide about layouts a timeline."
- "Tighten every claim to one line."
- "Split the next slide in two."

You can keep typing while the agent works. Changes in different places
combine by themselves; if you both change the same sentence, you choose.

## Read the whole argument at once
### Story shows every title and claim in order

Open Story at the top left. Rewrite a claim right there, or drag a slide to a
new place. The bar along the bottom of the workspace is the same storyline,
one title per slide.

> [!TAKEAWAY]
> If the titles alone tell the story, the deck works.

## Check, present, share
### Three buttons take you from draft to link

:::: timeline
#### Check
The button that says No problems, or what to fix, slide by slide.

#### Present
The screen button at the top: full-screen slides. Escape brings you back.

#### Share
Send the deck to your team's project and get a link to it.
::::

## Now make your own
### Ask the agent: "Turn my notes into a deck"

Paste notes, attach a file, or describe in a sentence what you need to present
and to whom. You get a draft like this one, open in Story, and you keep
shaping it together.

> [!TAKEAWAY]
> Your next deck starts with the notes you already have.
